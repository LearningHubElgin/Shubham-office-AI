What goes into an AMC contract

An AMC (annual maintenance contract) is a promise for a year: the customer pays a fixed fee, and you keep the listed equipment working with planned service visits and breakdown repairs. Government tenders sometimes call the version that includes spare parts a CMC, a comprehensive maintenance contract. Whatever the trade — AC, RO water purifier, CCTV, solar, lift or computers — a good AMC contract answers the same questions in writing:

  • Who and when: both parties, a contract number, the start and end dates.
  • What exactly: each unit with its brand, model, serial number and where it is.
  • What is covered: labour only, or labour and parts — and which parts, gas and filters.
  • How often: the number of preventive visits and the months they fall in.
  • How fast: how soon a technician comes after a breakdown call, and in which hours.
  • What is not covered: misuse, voltage damage, outside repairs, shifting.
  • Money: the price before GST, the GST, when it is paid, and what happens when it is late.
  • The end: renewal, ending it early, liability, and where disputes go.

The format below covers all of these. Write every one down: what is left unwritten is what you and the customer argue about later.

ANNUAL MAINTENANCE CONTRACT (AMC)

This contract is made between:

The Service Provider: [Company name], [address], GSTIN [GSTIN], phone [phone], email [email].

The Customer: [Name or company], [address of the site], GSTIN [if registered], phone [phone].

1. Period

This contract runs for [12] months, from [start date] to [end date], both days included.

2. Equipment covered

This contract covers only the equipment listed in Schedule A, at the address given there. Equipment added later is covered from the day both parties sign an updated Schedule A, at the price agreed then. The Service Provider inspected the equipment on [date] and found it in working order, except: [none / list].

3. Type of cover (tick one)

☐ Comprehensive: labour for preventive visits and breakdown calls, and the spare parts listed in clause 6(a), at no extra charge.

☐ Non-comprehensive (labour only): labour for preventive visits and breakdown calls. Spare parts, gas and consumables are charged as in clause 6(b).

4. Preventive maintenance visits

The Service Provider will make [4] preventive visits in the period, in or around [month], [month], [month] and [month]. Each visit covers the checklist in Schedule B and is recorded in Schedule C. The Service Provider will fix the date with the Customer at least [2] days before. If the Customer cannot give access on the agreed day, the visit moves to a new date both agree; a visit not taken by the end of the period lapses.

5. Breakdown calls

The Customer may report a breakdown on [phone / WhatsApp number] between [9 am and 7 pm, Monday to Saturday, except public holidays]. A technician will attend within [24] working hours of the call, at sites within [city limits / __ km]. If a part must be ordered, the Service Provider will tell the Customer the expected date. Breakdown calls in the period: [unlimited / up to __]. If a repair takes more than [__] days because of the Service Provider, it will [lend a standby unit / add the days lost to the contract / none].

6. Spare parts and consumables

(a) Comprehensive cover includes these parts: [list, e.g. PCB, fan motor, capacitor, pump, sensor]. It does not include: [list, e.g. compressor, filters and membranes, gas beyond __ top-up(s), remote, batteries, plastic and body parts, hard disks].

(b) Under non-comprehensive cover, and for any part outside 6(a), parts are charged at [the price on the Service Provider’s price list, plus GST / no more than the MRP, which includes GST], only after the Customer approves the estimate.

(c) Parts fitted will be [new and genuine / of equal quality]. Parts taken out [are returned to the Customer / become the Service Provider’s].

(d) If the maker no longer supplies a part, the Service Provider will tell the Customer and offer an alternative with its price; it is not bound to supply a part that is not available.

7. What is not covered

(a) Damage from accident, misuse, theft, fire, water, lightning, pests or rodents, or natural events.

(b) Damage from voltage fluctuation, faulty wiring or earthing at the site, or [for a water purifier: input water beyond the purifier’s rated TDS].

(c) Repairs or changes made by anyone other than the Service Provider while this contract runs.

(d) Shifting, uninstalling or reinstalling the equipment, and civil, plumbing or electrical work outside the equipment.

(e) Equipment not listed in Schedule A.

(f) [Any other exclusion].

8. The Customer’s part

The Customer will give safe access at agreed times, provide a stable power supply and proper earthing, use the equipment as the maker’s manual says, report faults promptly, and not let anyone else repair the equipment while this contract runs.

9. Price, GST and payment

The contract price is ₹[amount] plus GST at [18]% (SAC [code]), a total of ₹[total]. It is payable [in full in advance on signing / in __ instalments on __], by [UPI / bank transfer / cheque / payment link]. The Service Provider will issue a GST tax invoice. If a payment is more than [15] days late, the Service Provider may pause visits after written notice, until it is paid.

10. Renewal

This contract ends on its end date unless renewed. The Service Provider will remind the Customer about [30] days before. The renewal price may change; the new price will be given in writing before renewal. If the contract lapses, the Service Provider may inspect the equipment, at its usual visit charge, before taking it under a new contract.

11. Ending the contract early

Either party may end this contract with [30] days’ written notice; email or WhatsApp counts as writing. If the Customer ends it, the Service Provider will refund [the fee for the unused months, less the cost of visits and parts already given / nothing]. If either party breaks this contract and does not put it right within [15] days of written notice, the other may end it at once.

12. Liability

The Service Provider’s staff will take reasonable care of the Customer’s property. The Service Provider’s total liability under this contract is limited to the price paid for the current period, and it is not liable for indirect loss, such as lost business, spoiled food, lost data or lost recordings, except where the law does not allow such a limit. The Customer is responsible for backing up its own data.

13. Events beyond control

Neither party is liable for a delay caused by events beyond its reasonable control, such as floods, storms, strikes, lockdowns or government orders. Visits held up this way will be made as soon as possible after.

14. Service reports

Each visit is recorded in a service report, on paper or digital, with the date, the technician, the work done and the parts used, confirmed by the Customer by signature or message.

15. Disputes

The parties will first try to settle any dispute by talking to each other. This contract is governed by the laws of India, and the courts at [city] have jurisdiction.

16. Whole agreement

This contract, with Schedules A, B and C, is the whole agreement between the parties. A change is valid only when both agree to it in writing.

Comprehensive vs non-comprehensive AMC

Clause 3 is the biggest choice in the contract. A comprehensive AMC includes labour and the spare parts the contract lists, for one fixed price. A non-comprehensive AMC — also called labour only — includes the visits and the repair work, and parts are charged when they are used.

Comprehensive AMCNon-comprehensive AMC
Preventive visitsIncludedIncluded
Breakdown calls (labour)IncludedIncluded
Spare partsIncluded, as listed in the contractCharged extra, after the customer approves the estimate
Consumables: filters, membranes, gas, batteriesOnly those the contract names; say how manyCharged extra
PriceHigher: you carry the cost of parts that failLower: the customer pays for parts
The customer’s bill in the yearKnown in advanceChanges with what breaks
SuitsOlder or business-critical equipment; customers who want one fixed costNewer equipment that rarely needs parts; customers who would rather pay for parts as needed

Price a comprehensive AMC from your own records: what parts failed on that kind of unit, at that age, last year. Leave expensive parts you cannot predict, like an AC compressor on an old unit, out of the cover — or price them in openly. Either way, the parts list in clause 6(a) decides who pays when something breaks, so be exact there.

How to fill in the AMC contract format

  • Contract number. Keep one series — AMC-0001, AMC-0002 — and put the same number on the invoice and every visit report.
  • Period. Write both dates. A year from 16 October ends on 15 October the next year, not on the 16th. For a contract of two or three years, write the price for each year.
  • Equipment. Inspect each unit before you sign and write its serial number. A unit that was already faulty should be repaired, at a charge, or listed in clause 2 as an exception.
  • Visits. Promise a number you can deliver and name the months. Spread them evenly, with one just before the busy season — for an AC, before summer.
  • Response time. Say working hours, not “24 hours”, and limit the distance. A promise you keep is worth more than a short one you miss.
  • Price. Write the price before GST, the GST and the total separately, and take it in advance where you can.
  • Renewal. Remind the customer before the end, and give a new price in writing before it applies.
  • Signatures. Both sides sign every page; each keeps a signed copy.

When visits are spread evenly, they fall due like this for a contract from 1 April to 31 March:

GST and SAC code on an AMC

An AMC is a maintenance and repair service, which GST lists under heading 9987, “Maintenance, repair and installation (except construction) services”. At the time of writing (October 2026) the rate on it is 18%: 9% CGST plus 9% SGST on a supply within your state, or 18% IGST on a supply between states. The rate for the central half is entry 25 of Notification 11/2017-Central Tax (Rate), and the rate changes of 22 September 2025 did not change that entry.

The full SAC (services accounting code) has six digits; how many of them your invoice must show depends on your turnover, so ask your CA. The maintenance and repair codes in heading 9987, from GST’s official list of services, include:

SACMaintenance and repair services of…
998713Computers and peripheral equipment
998715Electrical household appliances
998716Telecommunication equipment and apparatus
998717Commercial and industrial machinery
998718Elevators and escalators
998719Other machinery and equipment

Which code fits your contract — a home AC, an office CCTV system, a rooftop solar plant — is a question for your CA; once settled, use it on every AMC invoice. A software AMC is a different kind of service, IT support rather than repair of equipment, so ask your CA for its code too. Spare parts you sell separately, under a non-comprehensive AMC, are goods: bill them with their own HSN code and rate. If a comprehensive AMC’s price includes parts, ask your CA how to show it on the invoice. The rates change from time to time, so check with your CA before you rely on them.

AMC visit checklists: AC, RO, CCTV, solar and computers

Attach the checklist that fits as Schedule B, so the customer knows what a visit includes and the technician knows what to do. These are starting lists: follow the maker’s manual where it says more, and note readings in the visit report.

AC AMC: what each visit covers

  • Clean the air filters and check them for damage.
  • Clean the indoor coil and the outdoor coil; a wet (jet or foam) service before summer.
  • Clear and flush the drain pipe; check for water leaking indoors.
  • Check cooling at the vent and the gas pressure; top up gas only after the leak is found and fixed.
  • Check the fan motors, blower, capacitor and wiring; tighten loose terminals.
  • Check the remote, the display and the thermostat.
  • Check the outdoor unit’s stand, airflow around it and vibration.

For a comprehensive AC AMC, say whether the compressor, the PCB and gas are included, and how many gas top-ups.

RO water purifier AMC: what each visit covers

  • Measure the TDS of the input water and the purified water; note both.
  • Clean the pre-filter and the sediment housing; replace the sediment and carbon filters when due.
  • Check the membrane’s output and the purified water’s TDS; replace the membrane when due.
  • Check the UV lamp or UF stage, where fitted.
  • Check the pump, adapter (SMPS), solenoid valve, float and auto shut-off.
  • Check every connector and pipe for leaks; clean and sanitise the storage tank.
  • Check the flow of purified water.

Filters and the membrane are most of an RO AMC’s cost. Write which ones a comprehensive AMC includes and how many times a year, and the input water TDS the purifier is rated for.

CCTV AMC: what each visit covers

  • Check every camera’s live view, focus, angle and night vision.
  • Clean the camera lenses and housings.
  • Check the DVR or NVR: recording on every channel, the hard disk’s health and the days of recording kept.
  • Check the recorder’s date and time.
  • Check the power supply, adapters, connectors, cables and the UPS.
  • Check remote viewing on the customer’s phone.
  • Update the firmware where the maker has released an update; change any default password.

For a CCTV AMC, list every camera and recorder in Schedule A with its location, and say whether hard disks, adapters and cable runs (how many metres) are included.

Solar AMC: what each visit covers

  • Clean the panels with water and a soft brush or mop, as the panel maker advises, in the early morning or evening.
  • Look for cracked glass, burn marks, bird droppings and new shade on the panels.
  • Check the mounting structure for rust and loose bolts.
  • Check cables, connectors, the junction box, DC and AC isolators, MCBs and surge protection.
  • Check the earthing and the lightning arrester connections.
  • Check the inverter: display, error codes and ventilation; note the generation and compare with the last visit.
  • Note the net meter reading, where fitted.

For a solar AMC, say how many panel cleanings the price includes — customers often want cleaning more often than technical visits — and whether inverter repairs are yours or left to the maker’s warranty.

Computer and printer AMC: what each visit covers

  • Clean the dust from the CPU cabinet, its fans and vents, the keyboard and the monitor; check every fan runs.
  • Check the hard disk or SSD’s health and free space, and that system updates are current.
  • Check that the antivirus is active and up to date, where the customer has one.
  • Check the power supply, the UPS and its battery backup, the cables and the earthing.
  • For printers: clean the paper path and rollers, check the print quality and note the page count.
  • Check the network: LAN cables, the switch or router, and Wi-Fi on each machine.
  • Remind the customer to back up their data before any repair.

For a computer AMC, list every machine, monitor and printer in Schedule A with its serial number, and say whether toner and cartridges, software licences and data recovery are outside the cover.

FAQ

AMC contracts: questions service companies ask

What is an AMC contract?

An AMC contract is an annual maintenance contract: the customer pays a fixed yearly fee, and the service company keeps the listed equipment working with planned service visits and breakdown repairs. It names the equipment, the visits, the response time, what is and is not covered, the price with GST, and how it renews.

What is the difference between comprehensive and non-comprehensive AMC?

A comprehensive AMC covers labour and the spare parts the contract lists, for one fixed price. A non-comprehensive AMC covers labour only — the visits and the repair work — and parts are charged when they are used. Either way, write down exactly which parts and consumables, such as filters or gas, are included.

What is the GST rate on an AMC in India?

18% at the time of writing. An AMC is a maintenance and repair service under heading 9987, taxed at 9% CGST plus 9% SGST within a state, or 18% IGST between states. Spare parts sold separately are goods with their own rate. Rates change from time to time, so confirm with your CA before you bill.

What is the SAC code for an AMC?

An AMC for equipment falls under heading 9987, maintenance and repair. The six-digit code depends on the equipment: 998715 is for electrical household appliances, 998713 for computers, 998716 for telecommunication equipment and 998719 for other machinery and equipment. Ask your CA which code fits your contract, and use it on every invoice.

How many service visits should an AMC include?

As many as the equipment needs and you can deliver — four a year, one each quarter, is common for an AC or a CCTV system; an RO purifier needs them as often as its filters clog with that water. Spread them evenly, put one before the busy season, and write the visit months into the contract.

Does an AMC contract need stamp paper?

It may. Stamp duty on agreements is set by each state, so whether your AMC needs e-stamp paper, and how much, depends on where it is signed. Ask your lawyer once and use the answer for every contract. Either way, both sides should sign every page and keep a signed copy each.

Will a business customer deduct TDS from my AMC fee?

It may. A business customer may have to deduct TDS, tax deducted at source, before paying you for a service contract; whether it applies, and at what rate, depends on the payment and the income-tax law in force, so ask your CA. The tax deducted is not lost: it counts towards your own income tax.

Can I raise the AMC price at renewal?

Yes, if the contract allows it. Clause 10 of this format lets the renewal price change, with the new price given in writing before renewal. Tell the customer early, explain what changed, such as parts prices or an older unit, and inspect equipment whose contract lapsed before you cover it again.
