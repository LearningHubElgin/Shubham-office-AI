What is a builder demand letter?

A builder demand letter is the letter you send a flat buyer when a construction stage is complete and the instalment tied to it falls due. Under-construction flats are often sold on a construction-linked payment plan (CLP): a booking amount, a part when the agreement for sale is signed, then a share of the price as each stage — the foundation, each floor slab, the brickwork, the flooring — is finished. This builder demand letter format covers every one of those letters.

It has two readers: the buyer, who pays their own share, and — when there is a home loan — the buyer's bank, which releases its part of the loan against your letter and your architect's certificate. So this page has a letter for each, and the Word file has both.

Under the RERA Act, a buyer must pay in the manner and within the time the agreement for sale sets (section 19(6)). So a demand letter only ever asks for what that agreement's payment schedule says, and only once the stage is really done.

Every demand letter needs:

  • your letterhead — name, address, GSTIN, phone and email;
  • the date and a reference number;
  • the buyer and co-applicant, with their address;
  • the project, tower, flat number and the project's RERA registration number;
  • the date of the agreement for sale and the agreement value;
  • the stage completed and the date, exactly as the architect's certificate says;
  • the payment schedule: each stage, its share, the amount, what was received and what is due now;
  • this instalment, its GST, the total due and the due date;
  • the account to pay into, and how to tell you it is paid;
  • the late-payment interest clause of the agreement;
  • what is enclosed, and a signature.

Your agreement for sale decides the stages, the shares, the days to pay and the interest; the letter only repeats them. Where the land share and the building are sold under two agreements, as in Tamil Nadu's model forms, the stage payments are in the construction agreement's payment schedule (Schedule D in the model form), so quote that agreement instead.

Construction-linked payment plan table

The table below is the construction-linked payment plan in its usual shape. Copy your own agreement's schedule into it, stage for stage — never a different share in a letter than the one in the agreement. The shares here are an example only.

  • The first payment. The RERA Act does not let a promoter take more than 10% of a flat's cost as an advance or application fee before a written agreement for sale is signed and registered (section 13(1)).
  • The shares add up to 100% of the agreement value. GST is worked out on each instalment and shown on its own line, never folded into the share.
  • One schedule per project, done tower by tower. When the 3rd floor slab is done in Tower B but not in Tower C, only Tower B's buyers get the letter.
  • Keep government charges out. Stamp duty and registration are paid to the government; do not list them as an instalment of the agreement value.

A worked example. A flat with an agreement value of ₹60,00,000 is not an affordable flat for GST (it is above ₹45 lakh), so its GST is 5%. The 3rd floor slab at 10% is ₹6,00,000; GST at 5% is ₹30,000; the buyer pays ₹6,30,000. Work the share out to the rupee first, then the GST on it, so the letter and your books never differ.

GST on a builder's demand letter

Since 1 April 2019, a residential flat that a promoter sells before it is complete carries GST at these effective rates, without input tax credit:

The flatGST on each instalment
An affordable flat: carpet area up to 60 sq m in a metropolitan city or 90 sq m anywhere else, and a gross amount of ₹45 lakh or less 1%
Any other residential flat5%
  • Land is already allowed for. The notification taxes the value after land, and takes land to be one-third of the total amount: central tax is 3.75% (0.75% for an affordable flat) on the other two-thirds, with the same again as state tax. That works out to 5% (or 1%) of the total, which is how the letter shows it; half of it is CGST and half SGST. If your CA wants the invoice to show the land deduction instead, the tax is the same.
  • Metropolitan cities, as the notification names them: Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi NCR (Delhi, Noida, Greater Noida, Ghaziabad, Gurgaon, Faridabad), Hyderabad, Kolkata and Mumbai (the whole of MMR).
  • The ₹45 lakh counts everything you charge the buyer for the flat: construction, the land share, and other charges such as preferential location, development, parking and common facility charges.
  • No GST under these rates where the whole price is received after the completion certificate, or after first occupation if that comes earlier.
  • Older projects, and two agreements. A project begun before 1 April 2019 whose promoter chose to stay on the old rates (8% or 12%, with input tax credit) is taxed differently. And where the land share and the building are sold under separate agreements, as in Tamil Nadu's model RERA forms (an agreement for sale of the undivided share of land, and a construction agreement), ask your CA how the GST and the land deduction apply to each.
  • The tax invoice is separate. Under the CGST Act, for a continuous supply of services where payment is linked to completing an event, the invoice is issued on or before the day that event is completed (section 31(5)(c)). The demand letter asks for the money; ask your CA how you issue the invoice for each stage.

The GST rate changes of September 2025 left these two rates as they were. Rates do change, though: confirm your project's rate with your CA before the first letter of a new project.

Builder demand letter to the bank for home loan disbursement

A bank pays a home loan for an under-construction flat in parts, as the building goes up — and it goes by the work actually done, not by your payment schedule alone. For each release, banks' own disbursement guides ask for:

  • the builder's demand letter for that stage;
  • an architect's certificate that the stage is complete;
  • receipts for the buyer's own contribution — what they have paid you from their own money;
  • the builder's account details, as the payee;
  • for the last release, the completion certificate; and, where the bank asks, the approved plan and a no-objection certificate (NOC) from the builder.

The buyer asks their bank for the release, often online, with these attached. HDFC Bank, for one, has the borrower upload the builder's demand letter, the architect's certificate and receipts for their own contribution when they ask for a release, and pays the builder. Send this letter to the branch with a copy to the buyer, or give it to the buyer to upload.

What to attach: the architect's certificate

The architect's certificate is the proof a stage is done, and banks ask for it with every release. Get one per stage, per tower, dated, and quote its date in both letters. Under the Architects Act, 1972, only a person registered with the Council of Architecture may use the title "architect" (section 37), so the certificate carries their registration number.

The RERA Act already asks for such certificates: 70% of what buyers pay goes into a separate account in a scheduled bank, and money comes out of it only when an engineer, an architect and a chartered accountant in practice certify that it is in proportion to the work done (section 4(2)(l)(D)). Use the same stage dates for your demand letters, so the two records agree.

Also attach a statement of the buyer's account, and the tax invoice if you issue it separately. Site photos of the stage help a buyer living away from the city.

Common mistakes in builder demand letters

  • Asking before the stage is done. The date must match the architect's certificate. Banks go by the work actually done, so an early letter holds up the loan part, and the buyer stops trusting your letters.
  • A share that is not in the agreement. Copy the percentages from the agreement's payment schedule, and quote its clause or schedule number.
  • The wrong GST. 5% on an affordable flat, 1% on one that is not, GST worked on land again, or GST on a flat whose whole price came after the completion certificate. Settle the rate per project with your CA once.
  • An interest rate of your own. Under the RERA Act, a buyer who pays late owes interest at the rate the rules prescribe, and it is the same rate you would owe the buyer for your own default (sections 19(7) and 2(za)). Tamil Nadu's rules set it at the State Bank of India's highest marginal cost of lending rate plus 2% (rule 18). Quote the clause; do not invent a penalty.
  • No "received so far". Without the running total, the buyer and their bank cannot reconcile, and the bank asks the buyer, who asks you.
  • Missing names or numbers. The co-applicant left out, the loan account number wrong, no RERA number, no agreement date. Banks send these back.
  • One account today, another next month. Keep one account per project in every letter, and tell buyers in writing if it ever changes.
  • The bank's letter sent late. The buyer's own money is due on the date you set, but the loan part waits for your letter and the certificate. Send the bank's letter the same day as the buyer's.
  • Treating the buyer's TDS as a short payment. On a higher-value flat, the buyer may have to deduct income tax at source from each payment and pay it to the government. Credit it to their account when the TDS certificate comes, and ask your CA for the current rules.
  • Calling the letter the invoice. The GST invoice is its own document with its own rules; see the GST section above.

Got a demand letter from your builder? What to check

If you are the buyer, check these before you pay. If you are the builder, make sure your letter already answers them:

  • the stage is really done: the architect's certificate is attached and dated, and a site visit or photos agree;
  • the share and the amount match the payment schedule in your agreement;
  • the GST rate is right for your flat, and the GST is on its own line;
  • the account is the one in your agreement or the builder's earlier letters — if it has changed, confirm it by calling the builder's office on a number you already have, not one in the new letter;
  • with a home loan, your bank has the letter, the certificate and the receipts for what you paid yourself.

Keep every letter, certificate and payment proof together; your bank will ask for them. If something does not match, write to the builder before the due date and quote the clause of your agreement.

FAQ

Questions about builder demand letters

What is a demand letter from a builder?

A demand letter from a builder is the letter that asks a flat buyer to pay the next instalment once a construction stage, such as a floor slab, is complete. It names the flat, the stage, the amount with GST, the due date and the account to pay into. Banks use it to release home-loan instalments.

How much GST does a builder charge on an instalment?

For a flat sold before completion, GST is 5% of the instalment, or 1% for an affordable flat (carpet area up to 60 sq m in the six metro areas or 90 sq m elsewhere, and ₹45 lakh or less), without input tax credit. The land's share is already allowed for. Confirm your project's rate with your CA.

What does a bank need to release a home loan instalment?

Banks usually ask for the builder's demand letter for that stage, an architect's certificate that the stage is complete, receipts for what the buyer has paid from their own money, and the builder's account details as the payee. For the last instalment, some ask for the completion certificate. Check your bank's own list.

What interest can a builder charge on a late instalment?

Only what your agreement for sale and your state's RERA rules allow. Under the RERA Act, a buyer who pays late owes interest at the rate the rules prescribe, and it must equal the rate the builder pays a buyer for its own default. Tamil Nadu's rules set SBI's highest marginal cost of lending rate plus 2%.

How much can a builder take before the agreement for sale?

Not more than 10% of the flat's cost, as an advance or application fee, until a written agreement for sale is signed and registered — section 13 of the RERA Act. After that, every instalment follows the payment schedule in the agreement, which is why a demand letter quotes the agreement's date and clause.

Is a builder demand letter a GST invoice?

Not by itself. The demand letter asks for the money; the tax invoice is the GST document. Under the CGST Act, for a continuous supply of services where payment is linked to completing a stage, the invoice is due on or before the day the stage is completed. Ask your CA how your firm issues it.
